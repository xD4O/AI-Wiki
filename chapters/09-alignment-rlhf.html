CHAPTER 09 Modern AI

Alignment & RLHF

How we turn a raw language model into something helpful, honest, and harmless — and why this problem is harder than it looks.

9.1 The Alignment Problem

A pretrained LLM has learned the distribution of internet text. Ask it a question and it produces text that's statistically likely given the prompt — which is not the same as helpful, truthful, or safe. It might:

  • Confidently fabricate facts (hallucinate).
  • Produce harmful content because such content exists in training data.
  • Give a technically correct but useless answer.
  • Refuse benign requests because it's seen users being told "no".

Alignment is the engineering problem of shaping model behavior to match human intent. Anthropic's formulation — helpful, honest, harmless (HHH) — is the touchstone that most labs work toward.

9.2 RLHF — the Full Pipeline

Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback was the breakthrough (InstructGPT, 2022) that made ChatGPT possible. It has three stages on top of pretraining.

The RLHF pipeline — SFT for bootstrap, reward model as a learned judge, PPO to optimize against that judge.

9.3 Reward Models

A reward model is itself an LLM — usually the same architecture as the base — with a linear head that outputs a single scalar score. It's trained on preference pairs: humans see two completions and pick the better one. The model learns:

$$ L_{\text{RM}} = -\log \sigma\!\big(r_\theta(x, y_\text{win}) - r_\theta(x, y_\text{lose})\big) $$

This is the Bradley-Terry model: train the score so the preferred response's score is higher than the rejected one's.

9.4 PPO for Language Models

Proximal Policy Optimization maximizes reward while preventing the policy from drifting too far from $\pi_{\text{SFT}}$. The objective includes a KL penalty:

$$ J(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{y \sim \pi_\theta} \!\big[\, r_\theta(x, y) - \beta \, D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\pi_\theta(\cdot \mid x) \,\|\, \pi_{\text{SFT}}(\cdot \mid x)) \,\big] $$
Reward hacking

Without the KL penalty, the policy will exploit quirks in the reward model — generating gibberish that happens to score high. The KL term keeps it close to a sensible language model. Too little KL ⇒ collapse. Too much ⇒ no improvement.

9.5 DPO — Skipping the Reward Model

Direct Preference Optimization (Rafailov et al., 2023) proved that you don't actually need a separate reward model or PPO. You can derive a closed-form loss that directly optimizes the policy on preference pairs:

$$ L_{\text{DPO}} = -\log \sigma\!\left( \beta \log \frac{\pi_\theta(y_w \mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_w \mid x)} - \beta \log \frac{\pi_\theta(y_l \mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_l \mid x)} \right) $$

DPO is simpler, more stable, and almost always matches RLHF in practice. Most new open-model alignment pipelines use DPO (or its variants: IPO, KTO, ORPO).

from trl import DPOTrainer
from transformers import AutoModelForCausalLM, AutoTokenizer

policy_model = AutoModelForCausalLM.from_pretrained("your-sft-model")
ref_model    = AutoModelForCausalLM.from_pretrained("your-sft-model")   # frozen
tok          = AutoTokenizer.from_pretrained("your-sft-model")

# preference dataset: { "prompt": ..., "chosen": ..., "rejected": ... }
trainer = DPOTrainer(
    policy_model,
    ref_model,
    beta=0.1,
    tokenizer=tok,
    train_dataset=preferences,
    args=TrainingArguments(output_dir="dpo-model", learning_rate=5e-6, num_train_epochs=1),
)
trainer.train()

9.6 Constitutional AI & RLAIF

Collecting human preference data is slow and expensive. Constitutional AI (Bai et al., 2022 — the method behind Claude) replaces much of the human labeling with AI labeling, guided by a written "constitution" — a small set of principles (e.g., "prefer responses that are helpful without being harmful").

  1. Show the model its own response next to a constitutional principle.
  2. Ask it to critique and revise. This produces SFT data.
  3. Later, have one model rank two responses from another. This produces preference data.
  4. Use that data exactly like human preferences for RLAIF (RLHF with AI feedback).

Constitutional AI scales alignment. You can generate millions of preference pairs in a weekend instead of a quarter.

9.7 Red-teaming & Evaluation

Alignment isn't a one-shot process. Before and after each fine-tuning run you need to probe the model.

Red-teaming

Adversarial experts (and automated systems) try to elicit harmful outputs. Each success becomes a training example for the next iteration.

Capability evals

MMLU, GSM8K, HumanEval, MATH — standardized tests. Track over time to catch regressions from safety training.

Honesty evals

TruthfulQA, calibration benchmarks. Does the model say "I don't know" when it should?

Safety evals

Refusal rates on harmful prompts, jailbreak robustness, bias across demographics.

Alignment is open research

No one knows the "right" way to align a model. RLHF/DPO/CAI are the current state of the art, but fundamental problems — interpretability, scalable oversight, deceptive alignment — remain open. This is the most important open problem in AI.
