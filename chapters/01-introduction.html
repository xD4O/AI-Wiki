Introduction to AI

1.1 What is Artificial Intelligence?

Artificial Intelligence is the field of building computer systems that can perform tasks which normally require human intelligence — perceiving the world, reasoning about it, planning, and learning from experience. It's a broad umbrella that includes everything from the rules a chess engine follows to the billions of parameters in a modern language model.

The concise modern definition: AI is the study and engineering of systems that act rationally given their goals and knowledge. A system is more "intelligent" when it can handle a wider variety of environments with less hand-coding.

AI vs. ML vs. DL vs. LLMs

These terms are often confused. AI is the broad goal. Machine Learning is one approach to AI (learning from data). Deep Learning is one approach to ML (using multi-layer neural networks). LLMs are one application of Deep Learning (language).

1.2 A Brief History

AI has gone through three major eras, each powered by new ideas and hardware.

Three eras of AI research and the shifts that ended each one.

The pivotal moments: The 1956 Dartmouth Workshop coined the term "artificial intelligence." AlexNet in 2012 proved deep networks + GPUs could crush classical computer vision. The 2017 "Attention Is All You Need" paper introduced transformers, which became the backbone of every major model that followed — including GPT, Claude, and Gemini.

1.3 The Three Paradigms

All AI research ultimately falls into three paradigms that answer the question "how does the system know what to do?"

Symbolic

Rules & Logic

Humans hand-write rules. Good for tasks with clear logical structure (theorem proving). Brittle when the world is messy.

Statistical

Learn from Data

The system finds patterns in labeled examples. Works well when you have lots of representative data and good features.

Neural / Connectionist

Learn Representations

Deep networks learn their own features directly from raw data. Dominant paradigm since ~2012.

1.4 Narrow, General, and Super Intelligence

TypeDefinitionToday?
ANI (Narrow)Great at one specific task. Chess engines, image classifiers, voice assistants.✅ Everywhere
AGI (General)Matches human performance across essentially all cognitive tasks.🟡 Active research
ASI (Super)Exceeds the best human experts in every domain.🔴 Hypothetical

Frontier LLMs like Claude and GPT-4 are often called general-purpose because they handle wildly different tasks — but they're still ANI by strict definitions.

1.6 Setting Up Your Environment

You'll want Python 3.10+, a virtual environment, and a few libraries. This wiki uses NumPy, PyTorch, and Hugging Face transformers.

# Create and activate a virtual environment
python -m venv .venv
source .venv/bin/activate    # Linux/Mac
# .venv\Scripts\activate       # Windows

# Install the core stack
pip install numpy matplotlib scikit-learn
pip install torch --index-url https://download.pytorch.org/whl/cu121
pip install transformers datasets tokenizers accelerate

A tiny "hello AI" to verify your setup:

import torch

# Check PyTorch and GPU availability
print(f"PyTorch: {torch.__version__}")
print(f"CUDA available: {torch.cuda.is_available()}")
if torch.cuda.is_available():
    print(f"GPU: {torch.cuda.get_device_name(0)}")

# Your first tensor
x = torch.tensor([[1.0, 2.0], [3.0, 4.0]])
print(f"Tensor:\n{x}")
print(f"Mean: {x.mean():.2f}")
You're ready
