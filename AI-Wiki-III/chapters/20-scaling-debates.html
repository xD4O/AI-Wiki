CHAPTER 20Part VI · Big Picture

Scaling Debates & the Data Wall

Is scaling dead? Sutskever said pre-training "as we know it" will end. Hinton sounds the alarm. LeCun argues we have the wrong architecture. Amodei published AI 2027 timelines. Sutton's Bitter Lesson is being re-argued weekly. Who's right — and what evidence would settle it?

2020 Kaplan scaling laws 2022 Chinchilla 2022 Villalobos data-limit forecast 2024 Sutskever "pretrain ends" 2025-04 AI 2027 scenario 2025 Hinton AGI-soon warnings
🧒 ELI5 — explain like I'm five

For a decade, the recipe was simple: "make the model bigger, give it more text, it will get smarter." Most progress came from this. But by 2024-2025 we ran low on quality text on the internet — that's the data wall.

Some people say we'll smash through it (synthetic data, RL on verifiable tasks, distillation). Some say we already have, and inference compute is the new axis. Some say we hit it years ago and were just hiding it. And one well-respected scientist (LeCun) has been saying the whole approach is wrong from the start.

Nobody knows. This chapter maps who's saying what — and what would change anyone's mind.

20.1 Chinchilla & the 2024 Revisions

Hoffmann et al.'s Chinchilla (2022) corrected Kaplan's scaling laws: at fixed compute, more data per parameter is better than more parameters per data. The "Chinchilla-optimal" ratio is roughly ~20 tokens per parameter.

2024-2025 revisitations softened this. Compute is no longer fungible — inference compute and training compute trade off differently. For models that will run billions of inferences, you want a smaller model trained on more tokens (well past Chinchilla-optimal training cost) so the inference is cheap. The Llama-3 line was famously trained on 15T tokens for an 8B model — way past Chinchilla.

20.2 The Data Wall

Villalobos et al. (Epoch AI 2022) projected that high-quality public text would run out around 2026-2028 at then-current scaling rates. In 2026 we are at the wall:

The data wall, simplified. Quality public text grows linearly; demand grows exponentially. Crossover ~2026.

20.3 Synthetic Data & Distillation

Recipes that broke the wall in 2024-2025:

  • Phi line (Microsoft) — "textbooks are all you need." Generate millions of textbook-quality questions and answers from larger models, train smaller models on them. Phi-4 (2024) demonstrates the recipe at 14B with surprisingly competitive numbers.
  • Nemotron (NVIDIA) — large-scale synthetic instruction data.
  • Self-instruct + agentic bootstrapping — let agents generate problem-solution pairs verified by tools.
  • Distillation — train a small student on the soft probabilities of a big teacher. R1 distilled into 1.5–32B students retains most of the reasoning gain.

20.4 New Scaling Axes

"Compute" is no longer a single number. In 2026 four distinct axes are in play:

  1. Pretraining compute. Old axis. Slowing because of the data wall.
  2. Inference compute. "Think longer" (Ch 10). New axis. Fast-growing.
  3. RL compute. RL on verifiable rewards (Ch 09). Distinct from pretraining; not yet at its limits.
  4. Verifier compute. Bigger / better graders during RL. The hidden multiplier.

Scaling is alive (just multi-axis)

Pretraining slowed but inference compute, RL compute, and synthetic data are now adding capability faster than pretraining ever did. Capability is still on a steep curve; the engine just changed.

Scaling is plateauing

Each new axis has its own diminishing returns. RL on verifiable rewards plateaus on tasks without verifiers. Inference compute saturates. Synthetic data has quality ceilings. The aggregate slope is flattening even if individual axes look new.

❓ Open questions

  • What is the actual ceiling of synthetic-data + distillation pipelines?
  • Is "RL compute" still the same scaling story as "pretraining compute," or a fundamentally different curve?
  • What evidence would convince Sutskever vs. LeCun? (Probably nothing short of an architectural demo on either side.)
