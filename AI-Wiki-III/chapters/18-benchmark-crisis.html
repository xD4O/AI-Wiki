The Benchmark Crisis

MMLU, HumanEval, GSM8K, SWE-bench Verified — saturated, contaminated, or both. ARC-AGI-2, FrontierMath, HLE, METR's task-horizon curve are what serious teams now measure against. The meta-problem: every new benchmark is six months from being trained on.

2018 SQuAD saturates 2022 MMLU released 2024-09 FrontierMath 2024 Humanity's Last Exam 2025-03 METR task-horizon 2025 ARC-AGI-2 prize 2026 SWE-bench Pro replaces Verified
🧒 ELI5 — explain like I'm five

If we want to know how smart models are, we give them a test (a "benchmark"). The trouble: every test we create gets memorised by the next training run. Then the model "passes" the test by recall, not skill — like a student who somehow saw the answer key.

So researchers are inventing tests that are private (held back from training data, like FrontierMath), novel (unlike anything seen, like ARC-AGI), or realistic (whole agent runs that take hours, like METR's task-horizon study).

18.2 ARC-AGI-2 — Generalisation as the Test

François Chollet's ARC benchmark (2019) was small, hand-designed, and deliberately impossible to memorise: each task is a few input-output grid examples, and the test demands you infer the rule. Frontier 2024 LLMs scored ~10%; humans average ~85%.

2025's ARC-AGI-2 upped the ante and offered a $1M+ prize for substantial progress. The bet: real generalisation looks like ARC, not like MMLU.

18.3 FrontierMath & HLE

FrontierMath (Epoch AI 2024): PhD-level math problems, vetted by Fields-medal-class mathematicians, never published online. Frontier models in late 2024 scored ~2%; in 2026 the leaders are at ~25-30%. The benchmark works precisely because nobody can train on it.

Humanity's Last Exam (2024): cross-disciplinary expert questions, 3000+ items, also held out. Designed to have a long "ceiling" — saturating it would itself be a milestone.

18.4 METR Task-Horizon — Real Agent Autonomy

METR's 2025 paper (Kwa et al.) measures something different: how long a task can a model complete autonomously, vs. a human? They fit an empirical curve.

METR's headline: task-horizon (the duration of tasks a model can autonomously complete at human-comparable success rate) doubles roughly every 7 months. If the curve holds, "agent-day" tasks arrive late 2027.

18.5 Contamination & Goodhart

Two failure modes for any popular benchmark:

  • Contamination — the test set ends up in the model's training data, directly or via re-published derivatives.
  • Goodhart — once everyone optimises for the metric, the metric stops measuring what it used to measure.

The 2026 best-practice: hold-out test sets (FrontierMath), per-instance perturbations (GSM-Symbolic), and continually-refreshed live evaluations (LMArena, METR's running studies).

Benchmarks still inform

Carefully-curated, held-out benchmarks (FrontierMath, ARC-AGI-2) provide real signal. The METR task-horizon curve is genuinely informative about agent autonomy. We're not benchmark-blind.

Benchmarks are obsolete the day they ship

Every new benchmark gets ingested into next year's training corpus. Optimised-against benchmarks tell you about optimisation pressure, not capability. Real evaluation has to be live, secret, and adversarial.

❓ Open questions

  • What's the right "evaluator-of-record" (UK AISI, US AISI, METR)?
  • Can we build benchmarks whose generators grow with capability?
  • Is METR's "doubles every 7 months" robust outside coding agents?
