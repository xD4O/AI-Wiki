CHAPTER 19Part VI · Big Picture

AI for Science

The under-hyped superpower. AlphaFold rewrote structural biology. GraphCast and GenCast match weather forecasters at a fraction of the cost. AlphaProof reaches IMO silver. AI is changing how science gets done — quietly, and in every discipline.

2018 AlphaFold 1 2020 AlphaFold 2 2023-11 GraphCast (Science) 2023-11 GNoME (Nature) 2024-05 AlphaFold 3 (Nature) 2024-07 AlphaProof IMO silver 2025-2026 AI-for-bio Cambrian explosion
🧒 ELI5 — explain like I'm five

For decades, predicting how a protein folds was a famously hard problem. AlphaFold solved it well enough that biologists now mostly stopped doing the old experimental method.

The same pattern is repeating in weather (GraphCast), chemistry (GNoME for materials), math (AlphaProof for theorems). Each time: a learned model replaces a hand-crafted simulator at a tiny fraction of the cost, and the old discipline reorganises around what the model lets you do quickly.

This is the part of AI that may matter most over decades — quieter than chatbots, harder to demo, but rewriting science.

19.1 Biology — AlphaFold's Lineage

AlphaFold 2 (2020) solved the 50-year-old protein-folding problem to within experimental error. AlphaFold 3 (Nature 2024) generalised to any biomolecular interaction — proteins, nucleic acids, ligands, ions. The downstream effect: drug discovery, structural biology, and synthetic biology pipelines now assume AlphaFold-quality structure prediction as table stakes.

2025-2026 follow-ups: RoseTTAFold-AA, Boltz-1 (open-weight), and a wave of generative protein-design models (RFdiffusion, Chroma).

19.2 Mathematics — Lean + LLM

The AlphaProof + AlphaGeometry 2 system (DeepMind 2024) reached silver-medal performance at the International Math Olympiad. Recipe: an LLM proposes proof steps in Lean 4; the Lean type-checker accepts or rejects; reward signal flows from the checker.

Terence Tao publicly uses LLMs as a daily-driver tool for math research. Lean's library (mathlib) has crossed 1.6M lines of formalised math. The combination — humans + LLM + proof checker — looks increasingly like the future of working mathematics.

19.3 Weather & Climate

In two years a GNN replaced a half-century-old physics simulator at ~60× lower cost. The same pattern is repeating in molecular dynamics, fluid simulation, and quantum chemistry.

19.4 Materials & Chemistry

GNoME (Google DeepMind, Nature 2023) discovered ~380K stable crystals — equivalent to "800 years of human experimentation." The companion A-Lab (Berkeley) autonomously synthesised 41 of them in 17 days. This is the autonomous-discovery loop: model proposes, robot tests, model updates.

Equivariant neural surrogates (MACE, Allegro, EquiformerV2) now run molecular dynamics at near-DFT accuracy at 1000× the speed.

19.5 The "Scale Is All You Need" Claim, in Science

Two camps:

  • Architecture matters. AlphaFold 2 worked because of attention plus SE(3)-equivariance, plus the MSA, plus a custom recycling loop. GraphCast works because the GNN respects the spherical structure of Earth. Inductive bias is half the win.
  • Scale matters. Most of the recent progress comes from larger models on more data. The custom architectures helped, but a transformer trained on the same data closes most of the gap.

The honest answer is "both, depending on the science." Domains with strong physical priors (PDEs, molecules) benefit from inductive bias; data-rich domains with weak priors (proteomics, sequence) close to the LLM playbook.

Architecture matters

AlphaFold's MSA + recycling, GraphCast's icosahedral mesh, MACE's E(3)-equivariance — these aren't decoration. Strip them and quality collapses. Science models earn their bias.

Scale matters more

Frontier transformers retrained on protein sequences (ESM-3, 2024) approach AlphaFold-class performance without explicit equivariance. The bitter lesson holds in science too.

❓ Open questions

  • Do we need a general "AI scientist" or a portfolio of domain-specific tools?
  • How do you eval a model that proposes novel hypotheses — i.e. that no human can grade in advance?
  • Will the AI-for-science feedback loop (model proposes → robot tests → model updates) close to a true autonomous discovery cycle within 2026-2030?
