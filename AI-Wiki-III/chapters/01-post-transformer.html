CHAPTER 01 Part I · Architecture

Why We're Looking Past Attention

Before we survey the successors, let's make the charges against the transformer concrete. What, specifically, is wrong at April-2026 scale — and what isn't?

2017 "Attention Is All You Need" 2020 GPT-3 scales 2022 Chinchilla 2023-12 Mamba-1 2024-05 Mamba-2 · SSD 2024-12 BLT 2025 o-series · R1 2026-04 you are here
🧒 ELI5 — explain like I'm five

Imagine you're in a classroom with 100 kids. Every time the teacher asks a question, every kid has to check with every other kid before answering. For 100 kids, that's 10,000 little conversations. For 1,000 kids, a million. This is how transformers ("attention") work — every word looks at every other word.

It works amazingly well, but it's expensive (costs scale with the square of the class size), and it has weird side-effects: the teacher chops words into odd pieces called tokens, so the model sometimes can't spell "strawberry" right. And every word carries around 16-bit decimal numbers when it could often get by with just three values: positive, zero, or negative.

Researchers are trying to replace attention with faster math (Mamba), get rid of the word-chopping (BLT), and simplify the numbers (BitNet). Volume III is a tour of what's working and what isn't.

1.1 The Setup — Why a Successor at All?

The transformer is an extraordinary piece of engineering. Two ideas — multi-head self-attention and the residual stream — took us from BERT to Claude Opus 4.7 in roughly eight years. Most of that time the main delta has been more of the same: wider models, more data, better training recipes.

And yet: by April 2026 four distinct architectural attacks are credibly in flight. Why?

Because attention has specific, quantifiable flaws that no amount of dumping compute on it will fix. Let's walk them.

1.2 The O(n²) Sin

Standard self-attention computes a similarity score between every pair of tokens. For a sequence of n tokens that's n² scores — both in compute and in the size of the attention matrix. The KV cache, which holds the keys and values of every past token so we don't recompute them at generation time, also grows linearly in n — which means at inference the memory bill is dominated by cache, not weights.

At n = 2K this is invisible. At n = 200K it is the bill.

Attention's quadratic cost is a hard wall at long contexts. SSMs trade it for an informational compression loss we'll characterise in Ch 02.

1.3 The Tokenization Original Sin

Transformers operate over tokens — roughly, chunks of 3–4 characters produced by a BPE tokenizer. Byte-pair encoding was a pragmatic choice in 2015. It has aged poorly.

Failure modeWhy it happens
"Count the rs in strawberry""strawberry" is one or two tokens, not ten characters. The model never sees the letters.
Arithmetic on big numbers"12345" might tokenize as one symbol; "54321" as another. Digit-level reasoning can't carry over.
Non-English & codeBPE was fit on English-heavy data. Thai, Tamil, emoji, rare code tokens use 2–5× more tokens per character — a cost and quality tax.
Reversal & substring ops"Reverse the word 'happiness'" requires operating inside a token. The model fakes it from memorised examples.
Spelling driftLow-frequency tokens are under-fit; the model "knows" a word exists but misspells it.

These aren't curiosities. They're the long tail that fine-tuning covers up and that real users hit every day.

1.4 The Dense-Matmul Tax

A transformer's FLOPs are dominated by large, dense matrix multiplies — MLP projections and attention projections — in BF16 or FP16. The weights themselves are the headline VRAM cost, and the matmuls the headline compute cost.

Two 2024 lines of work attack this in opposite directions:

  • Sparsity (fine-grained MoE, conditional routing) — do full-precision math, but only a fraction of it per token. Covered in Chapter 04.
  • Low precision (BitNet b1.58, matmul-free LMs) — use trivial arithmetic (ternary weights, pure adds) on every parameter. Also Chapter 04.

1.5 The Reasoning Gap

Frontier LLMs still fail on problems a competent 14-year-old would solve — specifically, problems that are perturbations of a training distribution rather than exact matches. Apple's GSM-Symbolic (2024) replaced numbers in GSM8K with new numbers and same-structure names; frontier models dropped 5–25 points. The argument is not "the model is bad"; it's "the model's generalisation looks template-matching more than reasoning."

We return to this debate in Chapter 12 with both sides' strongest evidence. For now, file it under "things attention plus more data hasn't obviously solved."

1.6 The 2026 Landscape

The post-Transformer landscape, April 2026. Each branch is an explicit answer to one of the sins above.
The transformer is not dying

Reports of the transformer's death are greatly exaggerated. Every frontier model shipping in April 2026 is still a transformer or a transformer-hybrid. Parts I and II of this volume are about the ideas that are already winning inside those hybrids — and the ones that are credibly trying to replace them whole.

Case for replacement

"Attention is quadratic, tokenization is broken, and dense matmul is wasteful. Every one of those costs has a working attack in 2026. A post-Transformer stack is happening whether you like it or not."

— Albert Gu, Tri Dao, Meta FAIR (BLT), Microsoft (BitNet)

Case for evolution

"Every proposed replacement so far has shipped as a hybrid. The attention block is still there because it is the best known in-context learning primitive. You don't replace a primitive by committee — you compete on benchmarks."

— most production frontier stacks (Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, Mistral) as of April 2026

❓ Open questions

  • Is the reasoning gap an architectural problem or a training-data problem?
  • Can any single architecture win all four attacks (attention, tokenization, matmul, reasoning) at once, or is the hybrid always optimal?
  • At what context length does the quadratic wall actually start mattering to users, in 2026 dollars?
