CHAPTER 04Part I · Architecture

The Sparse Revolution

Four parallel attacks on the dense-matmul tax: fine-grained MoE, BitNet b1.58, matmul-free LMs, and discrete diffusion for text. All say: spend compute where it matters, not everywhere.

2017 Sparse MoE (Shazeer) 2024-01 DeepSeek-MoE 2024-02 BitNet b1.58 2024-06 MatMul-Free LM 2024-10 SEDD (discrete diffusion) 2025 Mercury (Inception)
🧒 ELI5 — explain like I'm five

Think of a big language model as a kitchen with 1,000 chefs. Every dish, every chef helps — wildly expensive.

Fine-grained MoE: 1,000 chefs, but only 8 specialists work on any given dish. The router picks the right eight.

BitNet b1.58: every chef is allowed to answer only "yes", "no", or "meh" (-1, 0, +1). Turns out, surprisingly, a kitchen of 1,000 three-word chefs can still cook.

Matmul-free: throw out the multiplication entirely and use addition and routing. Cheap hardware, same dish (at least at small scale).

Discrete diffusion: instead of writing a sentence word-by-word, start with 20 blanks and fill them all in together, sharpening over a few rounds. Parallel instead of serial.

4.1 Fine-Grained MoE

A Mixture-of-Experts layer replaces a single MLP with N parallel MLPs and a router that picks k of them per token. In 2017 vanilla MoE, N was ~16 and k was 2. Fine-grained MoE (DeepSeek-MoE, 2024) flips the ratio: N in the hundreds, k around 8, plus a couple of shared experts that every token hits. Effective specialisation, better load balance, cleaner training dynamics.

Fine-grained MoE: many small experts, a few shared experts every token sees, a low-k router. Frontier open models (DeepSeek-V3, Kimi K2, Qwen 3 MoE) all share this pattern.

4.2 BitNet b1.58 & Matmul-Free LMs

BitNet b1.58 (Microsoft 2024) restricts weights to three values: \(\{-1, 0, +1\}\). That is 1.58 bits of information per weight (\(\log_2 3\)). Because every weight is a choice between three additions, the forward pass has no multiplies — only add/subtract/skip.

It shouldn't work. It mostly does. BitNet b1.58 3B matches Llama 2 7B on perplexity at ~1/10 the energy. The story holds up to 13B in follow-up papers; 70B is still pending independent replication as of April 2026.

MatMul-Free LMs (Zhu et al. 2024) go further — replace linear layers entirely with ternary "BitLinear" and the attention block with a routing primitive. The resulting model runs fast on FPGAs and GPUs-without-tensor-cores.

4.3 Discrete Diffusion for Text

Autoregressive generation decodes one token at a time — inherently sequential. Diffusion generates all tokens in parallel and sharpens over a small number of steps. In 2024 this crossed from "research curiosity" to "production-viable" with SEDD (Lou et al.) and Mercury (Inception Labs, 2025), which demonstrated 5-10× latency improvements at competitive quality.

The open question: can discrete diffusion match autoregressive on in-context learning and long-form coherence? As of April 2026, short-form wins are real; long-form is still gapped.

4.4 The Unifying Theme

MoE, BitNet, matmul-free, diffusion-for-text look like four unrelated papers. They share one thesis: the dense, FP16, autoregressive transformer spends too much compute per token. Each attacks a different axis:

  • MoE — less compute by routing.
  • BitNet — less compute by precision.
  • MatMul-free — less compute by operation.
  • Diffusion — less compute by parallelism.

A production frontier model in 2027 may well combine all four.

Sparse is the future

Open-weight frontier models (DeepSeek-V3, Kimi K2, Qwen 3) are all MoE. Closed frontier quietly followed. BitNet's energy story makes on-device AI tractable. Once any of these cross 70B with matched quality, the dense baseline is legacy.

Sparse is training pain

MoE routing is unstable; BitNet training is brittle; matmul-free has no hardware moat yet; diffusion-for-text plateaus on long documents. Every one of these has a research paper and a messy reality. Dense transformers at frontier scale still beat them on most evals.

❓ Open questions

  • Does BitNet b1.58 replicate at 70B / 405B, or does the low-precision story break at scale?
  • Can discrete diffusion match autoregressive on reasoning benchmarks?
  • Is there a coherent "sparse + low-bit + diffusion" architecture, or do they work against each other?
