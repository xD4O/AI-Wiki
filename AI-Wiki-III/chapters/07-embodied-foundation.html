CHAPTER 07Part II · World Models

Embodied Foundation Models

Robotics' GPT moment, and why 2024–2026 is when it arrived. RT-2 made action a language. π0 and GR00T made that language cross hardware. We're one recipe away from a foundation model for manipulation.

2023-07 RT-2 (Google) 2023-10 Open X-Embodiment 2024-03 DROID dataset 2024-10 π0 (Physical Intelligence) 2025 π0.5 · π2 · GR00T N1 (NVIDIA) 2026 humanoid-policy race
🧒 ELI5 — explain like I'm five

For a long time, every robot had its own custom brain. A robot vacuum couldn't help a robot arm, a robot arm couldn't help a humanoid. Training data was a few hundred demonstrations per robot.

Now, big teams are collecting data from many different robots doing many different things and training one shared brain that works across them. The model eats camera images and outputs motor commands — the same way language models eat words and output words. That's what "embodied foundation model" means: one brain for many bodies.

7.1 The Robotics Data-Scaling Problem

Language has the internet. Robotics has… each lab's drawer of demonstrations. The 2023–2024 breakthrough was consolidating many small datasets into one big one:

  • Open X-Embodiment (2023) — 1M+ episodes from 22 robots, 21 institutions.
  • DROID (2024) — 76K manipulation demos from 564 scenes.
  • RT-X — Google's cross-embodiment training recipe on OXE.

Once you have one big dataset, you can train one big model.

7.2 RT-2 — Action as Language

Google's RT-2 (2023) made the key conceptual move: discretise robot actions into a language-model vocabulary. Delta-XYZ becomes a token range; gripper open/close becomes a token; timing becomes tokens. Then fine-tune a large vision-language model on internet data + robot demonstrations, and action generation becomes next-token prediction.

Results: web-scale pretraining transfers to robotics. An RT-2 policy trained to "pick up objects" generalises to "pick up the red thing on the left" because the VLM understood "red" from its internet training.

RT-2 treats robot actions as language tokens. Web pretraining transfers; the robot "speaks" a motor-command language.

7.3 π0 and Flow-Matched Action

Physical Intelligence (Chelsea Finn + Sergey Levine + others, est. 2024) released π0 in late 2024 and π0.5 / π2 in 2025. Their big move: instead of tokenising actions, predict them with flow matching — a diffusion-like generative model over continuous action trajectories.

Advantages: smoother trajectories, better cross-embodiment transfer, no vocabulary tuning. π2 runs policies across dozens of robot morphologies (arms, mobile manipulators, humanoids) with one checkpoint.

7.4 NVIDIA GR00T — Humanoid Foundation Policies

NVIDIA's GR00T N1 (2025) and N2 target a specific bet: humanoids will be the general-purpose robot body, because our factories and homes are human-shaped. GR00T pairs a high-frequency motor-control backbone with a slower VLM-style planner, trained on human-video + teleoperation + sim.

By April 2026 GR00T and π2 are the two most-cited examples of the embodied-foundation-model story actually shipping.

7.5 What's Still Broken

  • Dexterity. Pick-and-place: solved. Inserting a USB, tying a shoe, peeling a fruit: not yet.
  • Long-horizon tasks. "Load the dishwasher" involves 50+ primitive steps; error accumulates fast.
  • Sim-to-real. Closed under strong domain randomization; open for fine motor and visual surprises.
  • Data hungry. Even with OXE + DROID, robotics is O(10⁶) episodes vs. O(10¹²) tokens for language. The scaling curve is not yet saturated but also not yet cheap.
Tokens-for-actions works

RT-2 / π-series / GR00T all work. One policy trained on pooled data beats hand-crafted single-task policies. This is exactly what happened in vision (ResNet) and language (GPT-3). We're on the curve.

Discretisation is a compromise

Continuous control wants continuous heads; discretising actions hurts precision. The flow-matching π-line already abandoned it. The wins attributed to "tokens-for-actions" may just be wins from scale.

❓ Open questions

  • Does one policy per embodiment beat one policy across embodiments, once you match data? (Unclear as of April 2026.)
  • How much synthetic data from video + simulation (see Ch 06) can substitute for teleoperation?
  • At what robot count does the data flywheel become self-sustaining?
