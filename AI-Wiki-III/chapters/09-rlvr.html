CHAPTER 09Part III · Reasoning & Training

RL on Verifiable Rewards

The 2025 shift in training. When the reward is a grader that cannot be gamed — a Python interpreter, a proof checker, a unit test — RL becomes the best tool we have. DeepSeek-R1 and the o-series are the recipe.

2022 RLHF (InstructGPT) 2023-05 "Let's Verify" (PRMs) 2024-02 GRPO (DeepSeekMath) 2024-09 o1 preview 2025-01 DeepSeek-R1 2025 R1 Nature paper
🧒 ELI5 — explain like I'm five

Old way of teaching an LLM: show it two answers and ask a human, "which is better?" The human's taste becomes the reward. Humans are inconsistent, expensive, and the model eventually learns to game their taste (called "reward hacking").

New way (RLVR): only teach on problems where we have a cheat-proof grader — math problems where the answer is checkable, code where tests pass or fail, schema-constrained outputs that either parse or don't. The reward is the grader's output. Can't be gamed, can run a million times a day, and the model improves on the skill the grader measures.

Result: models that got dramatically better at math and coding in 2024-2025, not by scaling, but by this new training step.

9.1 The RLHF Plateau

RLHF trained a reward model on human preference pairs. The reward model was then the signal for PPO. By 2024 three problems were acute:

  • Reward hacking. Models learned to exploit quirks of the reward model (verbose answers, sycophancy, confident tone).
  • Distribution shift. The RM was trained on offline preferences, but PPO samples went off that distribution.
  • Cost. Human labelling at frontier scale is a seven-figure line item.

RLVR sidesteps all three when the task has a verifier.

9.2 DeepSeek-R1 — The Recipe

The R1 pipeline: minimal SFT seed, heavy RLVR, rejection-sampling fine-tune, distill to smaller students.

9.3 GRPO vs. PPO vs. RLOO

GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimisation, DeepSeekMath 2024) is PPO without the value network. Instead of learning V(s) to compute advantages, you sample G completions per prompt, compute rewards, normalise by the group mean and std. Cheap, stable, and surprisingly effective for verifier-based rewards.

$$\hat A_{i,t} = \frac{r_i - \text{mean}(r_1, \ldots, r_G)}{\text{std}(r_1, \ldots, r_G)}$$

RLOO (Ahmadian et al. 2024) is the same idea with leave-one-out normalisation. Similar story.

PPO with value model still wins on dense-reward tasks, but verifier rewards are sparse (pass/fail), making GRPO's simplicity a feature not a bug.

9.4 Process Reward Models vs. Outcome Reward Models

  • ORM — score only the final answer. Cheap; misses local errors early in a long chain.
  • PRM — score each reasoning step. More expensive; finds which step went wrong. OpenAI's "Let's Verify Step by Step" (2023) showed PRMs dramatically improve math performance at inference by guiding tree search.

9.5 Tool-Use RL

Extending RLVR: reward comes not from a text grader but from a tool's output. Did the generated Python actually compile and pass tests? Did the SQL return the expected rows? Did the browser click produce the expected page? Every tool with a pass/fail signal is a reward source. This is why agent RL is a research frontier in 2026.

RL teaches reasoning

R1 emerged with backtracking and self-correction during pure RLVR — behaviours not present in the base model. Something genuinely new is being learned, not just amplified.

— DeepSeek, OpenAI (o-series)

RL only surfaces latent reasoning

The base model already contains the reasoning ability; RL just re-weights it. Distilling R1 into a small SFT student from reasoning traces alone gets most of the gain. If this is right, the scarce resource is not RL but good base models.

— the distillation-is-all-you-need camp, 2025 ablation papers

❓ Open questions

  • Does RLVR generalise to unverifiable tasks (creative writing, nuanced judgement) via transfer?
  • How much of R1's gain survives without the huge DeepSeek-V3 base? (Open-weight 1.5B students are mediocre; 32B are strong.)
  • Is the reasoning learned genuinely novel, or compression of existing solutions?
