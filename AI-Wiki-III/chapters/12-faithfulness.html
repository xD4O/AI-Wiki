CHAPTER 12Part III · Reasoning & Training

The Faithfulness Crisis

Does chain-of-thought reflect actual reasoning, or is it a plausible-sounding post-hoc rationalisation? Apple's GSM-Symbolic and Anthropic's faithfulness studies have reopened the question with both sides' strongest evidence.

2023-05 Turpin et al. — "Don't Always Say What They Think" 2024-10 Apple ML — GSM-Symbolic 2024-2025 Anthropic faithfulness follow-ups 2025 o-series self-consistency studies
🧒 ELI5 — explain like I'm five

You ask a model: "If Tom has 5 apples and gives away 2, how many does he have?" The model says: "Tom has 5 apples. He gives away 2. So he has 3." Sounds like reasoning.

Now you change it: "Tom has 5 apples and gives away three apples, one each to his sister, brother, and cousin." The model sometimes still says "3" — because it was pattern-matching the shape of the problem, not actually doing subtraction.

That's the "faithfulness" worry: the model says it reasoned, but the reasoning might be a story told after the fact. Two of the biggest AI labs have spent 2024–2025 arguing about how bad this is.

12.1 GSM-Symbolic — Apple's Gauntlet

Mirzadeh et al. (Apple ML 2024) took the standard GSM8K math benchmark and perturbed each problem: change names, swap numbers, add irrelevant clauses that don't affect the answer. The structure of each problem is identical; only surface details change.

Frontier model accuracy dropped by 5-25 points depending on the perturbation. On one variant ("no-op clause" — add a clause that doesn't change the math), a handful of frontier models actually "subtracted" the irrelevant number — strong evidence of template-matching rather than symbolic manipulation.

GSM-Symbolic's no-op clause. The colour fact is mathematically irrelevant, but frontier models sometimes fold it into their "chain of thought."

12.2 Anthropic's CoT Studies

Turpin et al. (2023) showed that when you bias a prompt toward a wrong answer (by permuting multiple-choice labels so the "correct" label is always "B"), models produce confident chain-of-thought that arrives at "B" — even on problems where B is wrong. The CoT is post-hoc rationalisation of a decision made by other means.

Anthropic's 2024-2025 follow-ups used mechanistic interpretability (Ch 16) to trace which internal activations actually caused the answer. Finding: the "reasoning" tokens in CoT are often not on the causal path.

12.3 What is Reasoning, Anyway?

Two incompatible definitions animate this debate:

  • Symbolic / formal. Reasoning means applying explicit rules to symbolic representations — the definition that Mitchell, Marcus, LeCun use. By this definition, LLMs reason weakly.
  • Behavioural. Reasoning means generalising beyond memorised patterns in ways that track relevant structure. By this definition, frontier LLMs reason — imperfectly, but nontrivially.

The two camps are not going to converge until someone proposes a decisive test. GSM-Symbolic is the closest we have; the o-series' handling of it is improving but not solved.

12.4 What Would Settle It?

Proposed tests, not yet run at frontier scale:

  • OOD compositional generalisation at 5+ hops on structurally-new problem families.
  • Interpretability-grounded faithfulness — the chain-of-thought is causally on-path per activation patching.
  • Adversarial perturbation distances — how small a semantic-preserving edit breaks the answer?
Frontier LLMs reason

o-series and R1 solve problems their base models demonstrably cannot. The reasoning may be imperfect and unfaithful in detail, but it tracks the structure of novel problems better than pattern matching would predict. GSM-Symbolic drops are real but small, and they close with scale.

— Sutskever, Amodei, most OpenAI/Anthropic research

It's elaborate pattern matching

The faithfulness failures are not a bug, they're a clue: the "reasoning" in CoT is a story, not a mechanism. Perturbations that don't change meaning change the answer; that's template matching. Scaling improves performance; it doesn't prove reasoning.

— Marcus, Mitchell, LeCun (Apple paper effectively agrees)

❓ Open questions

  • Is CoT faithfulness improvable through training, or is it an inherent property of autoregressive generation?
  • Do RL-trained reasoning models (R1, o-series) actually have more faithful CoT, or just more useful CoT?
  • What's the right benchmark for "real reasoning" that doesn't saturate in 12 months?
