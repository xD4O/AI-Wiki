CHAPTER 05Part II · World Models

JEPA — LeCun's Non-Generative Bet

Yann LeCun has argued for five years that autoregressive LLMs are a dead end. His alternative, the Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture, predicts representations — not pixels, not tokens. By 2025 V-JEPA 2 is producing results worth arguing about.

2022 LeCun "Path" paper 2023 I-JEPA (images) 2024-04 V-JEPA (video) 2025 V-JEPA 2 (video + planning) 2025-12 LeCun's departure from Meta AI widely reported
🧒 ELI5 — explain like I'm five

When you toss a ball, your brain predicts where it will land — but not by imagining every pixel of the ball mid-flight. It predicts an abstract idea of the landing spot.

Most AI video models today are like an artist who paints every frame of the ball's flight in full detail — called generative. JEPA is like the brain: it predicts a small summary ("ball will be here-ish"), saving effort on the details that don't matter.

LeCun thinks this is how intelligence will actually work, and that "paint every pixel" is a wasteful dead end. JEPA is his bet.

5.1 Why LeCun Dismisses Generative LLMs

LeCun's argument, stated compactly: an autoregressive model makes one probabilistic choice per token. If each choice has even a tiny chance of leading off-distribution, the compounded probability of a coherent 1000-token answer goes to zero. Ergo, scaling LLMs cannot reach robust reasoning — the math is against it.

Critics respond: empirically, scaling has worked, so the argument is wrong somewhere. LeCun responds: it's worked on shallow tasks; on deep reasoning it manifestly hasn't (see Ch 12, the Faithfulness Crisis). The debate is live and the evidence is still being collected.

5.2 Energy-Based Models

JEPA sits in the framework of energy-based models (EBMs). An EBM assigns low energy \(E(x, y)\) to compatible (x, y) pairs and high energy to incompatible ones. No explicit probability distribution; no requirement to generate samples. To "use" the model, minimise energy with respect to what you care about.

This matters because a generative model must model the full distribution of y given x — including all the irrelevant details. An EBM only has to distinguish good from bad. On high-dimensional signals (video, images), that's much cheaper.

5.3 The JEPA Architecture

JEPA: a student encodes the context, a teacher (EMA of student, frozen gradient) encodes the target, and a predictor learns to bridge them. The loss is representation-space L2, not pixel reconstruction.

5.4 V-JEPA 2 — Evidence It's Working

V-JEPA 2 (Meta FAIR 2025) scaled the idea to video. The headline results:

  • Matches or beats previous best on video classification & action recognition with fewer labelled data points (representation learning wins when labels are scarce).
  • Zero-shot action anticipation — predicting what a person will do next — on real video.
  • Down-stream robotic manipulation: feed V-JEPA 2 a plan prompt, the learned world model rolls out imagined futures, and a low-level controller picks the best.

The last result is the most important, because it closes the loop on LeCun's original claim: a model that predicts usefully in representation space can be a planning primitive.

5.5 Can JEPA Do Language?

The open question everyone asks: if JEPA is so great, why isn't there a JEPA chatbot? Two honest answers:

  • Language tokens are already discrete and low-dimensional. The pixel-prediction problem that makes JEPA shine on video doesn't hurt LLMs the same way.
  • JEPA-style training may still be the right representation for *input* to a language model (grounding via video); it is not obviously a better language-generation primitive.

LeCun's framing: language intelligence is a tiny corner of real intelligence; the rest of the problem is representation learning on sensory data. By that framing, JEPA is complementary, not competitive, with LLMs. It's one of the reasons the debate won't settle cleanly.

The JEPA case

Generative LLMs compound errors token-by-token; representation-space prediction doesn't. Humans don't imagine pixels when they plan. Every major advance in perception has come from self-supervised representation learning, not from generation. V-JEPA 2's robotic results vindicate the thesis.

— Yann LeCun, Mahmoud Assran, Meta FAIR

The generative case

LeCun's compounding-error argument is a theoretical claim that empirics have undermined: frontier LLMs routinely produce coherent 10K-token answers. Scaling solved it. JEPA is a principled research program, but no JEPA-native language model has shipped. Until that changes, LLMs are the default.

— Ilya Sutskever, Dario Amodei, most OpenAI/Anthropic senior research

❓ Open questions

  • Can JEPA's planning primitive be scaled to hours-of-video horizons without falling into fuzzy-representation collapse?
  • What's the right architecture for a JEPA × transformer hybrid that handles both perception (JEPA) and language (LLM)?
  • If JEPA representations are so useful, why haven't they replaced CLIP-style encoders in multimodal LLMs yet?
