CHAPTER 21Part VI · Big Picture

Compute, Energy, Geopolitics & the Path to AGI

Closing the volume. Chips, kilowatt-hours, sovereign infrastructure, regulation. The economic and political constraints that decide which architectures (Parts I-IV) actually ship — and the honest map of who disagrees about timelines.

2022 CHIPS Act 2023 US AI Executive Order 2024-08 EU AI Act in force 2024 UK + US AISIs stand up 2025 sovereign compute everywhere 2026 nuclear PPA boom for hyperscalers
🧒 ELI5 — explain like I'm five

Big AI models need three things: chips (mostly TSMC), electricity (a small country's worth), and permission (regulators).

So the question "when will AGI arrive?" isn't only about research. It's also: when can we build chip factories outside Taiwan? Where does the next 100GW of electricity come from? Will governments allow it? Smart people disagree on all of these.

This chapter is the honest map: who's saying what, why, and what would change anyone's mind.

21.1 Chip Supply Chains

Frontier training runs depend on:

  • TSMC for fabrication (95%+ of leading-node logic).
  • ASML for EUV lithography (sole vendor).
  • Nvidia for design + CUDA software stack (~90% AI accelerator share).
  • HBM (SK Hynix, Samsung, Micron) for memory — the bottleneck since 2023.

US export controls (2022, 2023, 2024) progressively restricted advanced AI chips to China. China responded with intensified domestic capacity (SMIC), Huawei Ascend chips, and large open-weight model releases (DeepSeek, Qwen, Kimi). The cat-and-mouse continues; the technical capability gap has narrowed in 2025-2026.

21.2 Power — Where the Watts Come From

A 2026 frontier training run consumes ~100 GWh, equivalent to a city of 10K people for a year. Inference at scale is now larger than training in aggregate. The 2024-2026 power story:

  • Nuclear PPAs — Microsoft restarted Three Mile Island; Amazon, Meta, Google signed multi-GW SMR deals.
  • Geothermal & hydro — new datacentres co-located with renewable supply.
  • Behind-the-meter natural gas — controversial but fast.

The IEA forecasts AI compute at 5-10% of US grid by 2030. Power, not chips, may be the binding constraint.

21.3 Sovereign Compute

Every major economy is building national-scale compute infrastructure:

  • EU AI Gigafactories — multi-billion-euro public-private programmes.
  • UAE Stargate, Saudi HUMAIN — Gulf-state hyperscalers funded by sovereign wealth.
  • India IndiaAI Mission — 10K-GPU public allocation programme.
  • Japan, South Korea, France — domestic frontier-model players (Sakana, Upstage, Mistral) with state backing.

21.4 Labor — What's Actually Happening

Distinguish public discourse from data:

  • Coding — measurable productivity gains (~30-50% on routine tasks); junior-coder hiring has slowed at AI-leading firms; senior-engineer time spent on review and architecture is up.
  • Customer support — AI handles ~50% of tier-1 tickets; teams reorganised, not eliminated, in most large enterprises.
  • Creative — design, copywriting, illustration freelancers report mixed effects: more clients but lower per-task pay.
  • Knowledge work generally — not the mass displacement some feared, not the productivity miracle others promised. Quietly reshaping every desk job.

The honest answer in 2026: real, but slower and more uneven than dramatic forecasts predicted.

21.5 Regulation

  • EU AI Act (2024-2025 phased) — risk-based; foundation-model obligations include transparency, evals, copyright disclosure.
  • UK AISI & US AISI — voluntary pre-deployment evals of frontier models. The "evaluator-of-record" question is partly answered.
  • US Executive Orders — successive administrations have written, revised, and walked back AI executive orders. The federal landscape is uncertain through 2026.
  • State-level (US) — California's SB 1047 (vetoed 2024, replaced by SB 53 line); New York and Colorado moving on disclosure / liability.
  • Asia — China's generative-AI rules (2023+) require pre-approval; Japan's "soft" approach; Singapore as model-evaluation hub.

21.7 What to Watch in 2027

  • METR's task-horizon curve. Does it stay on its 7-month-doubling fit? If yes: agent-day capability ~late 2027.
  • FrontierMath progress. If frontier models cross 50%, the "real reasoning" debate moves to a new venue.
  • RSP threshold crossings. Anthropic ASL-4, DeepMind FSF critical-capability, OpenAI Preparedness "high" — first time any are tripped.
  • Open-weight gap. If a Chinese open-weight model leads LMArena for a quarter, the global landscape changes.
  • BLT / Mamba at frontier scale. First non-transformer to make a frontier release.
  • Embodied AI. First humanoid policy that solves a useful real-world task end-to-end (laundry? warehouse pick?).
  • AGI definition. If a credible operationalisation of "AGI" emerges, the debate becomes empirical rather than theological.
