APPENDIX★ Use Cases · 2026

AI in the Wild — Use Cases by Domain

Where AI is actually being used across industries in April 2026. Organized by domain with concrete examples and the companies shipping them. Verifiable products only; no vaporware.

🧒 ELI5 — what is this?

Everyone wants to know: "so what is AI actually used for?" This page answers that question domain-by-domain. Healthcare, law, software, creative, robotics — sixteen industries, real products, honest assessment of what's working vs. what's still aspirational.

Each tile tells you: the tasks AI is handling, the companies doing it, and a caveat where relevant ("this works" vs "this is hyped but unreliable").

How to read the maturity tags
  • Shipped — real, measurable production usage at scale.
  • Emerging — working in pockets; incumbents piloting; not yet ubiquitous.
  • Experimental — demos exist; production deployments rare or narrow.

💻 Software Engineering shipped

The most mature AI adoption. Coding is where the flywheel runs fastest — code reviewers are LLMs, coders use LLMs, and every new LLM gets tested on code.

What AI is doing

  • In-editor completion — ghost-text suggestions line-by-line while you type.
  • Agent-mode composers — "make this feature work" — multi-file edit, run tests, iterate.
  • PR review — automated first-pass review on every pull request.
  • Autonomous issue-to-PR agents — given a ticket, produce a merged PR.
  • Bug bounty / security review — scanning for vulnerabilities at scale.

Tools shipping now: Claude Code · Cursor / Cursor Agent · GitHub Copilot + Copilot Workspace · Windsurf / Codeium · Devin (Cognition) · Replit Agent · Zed AI · Continue.dev · Aider

Survey data from 2025-2026 shows 70-90% of engineers at frontier-AI companies use AI coding tools daily; ~30-50% productivity gains on routine work, less on architectural tasks. Junior hiring slowed at AI-leading firms.

🩺 Healthcare & Medicine emerging

Real deployments in ambient scribing and radiology; clinical decision support still cautious. Drug discovery is the quiet revolution.

What AI is doing

  • Ambient clinical scribing — microphone in the exam room, LLM writes the SOAP note.
  • Radiology triage — flagging CT/MRI studies for urgent review; second-read on mammograms.
  • Medical Q&A — clinician copilots for evidence-based answers grounded in literature.
  • Drug discovery — AlphaFold structure prediction, generative protein design, autonomous labs.
  • Patient-facing intake & triage — symptom checkers with LLM-powered dialogue.

Tools shipping now: Nuance DAX / Dragon · Abridge · Suki · Nabla · OpenEvidence · Glass Health · Aidoc · Viz.ai · PathAI · Isomorphic Labs · Recursion

FDA still treats autonomous diagnosis as a medical device requiring clearance. Most deployments are copilot-not-oracle and keep a licensed human in the loop. Hallucination remains a safety blocker for direct patient-facing advice.

⚖️ Law & Legal shipped

One of the first verticals to see AI in daily use. Contract work, research, and discovery are being reshaped fastest.

What AI is doing

  • Contract review & redlining — flag unusual clauses, benchmark against playbooks.
  • Legal research — citation-aware case-law search replacing Westlaw / Lexis workflows.
  • Discovery / document review — classifying millions of pages in litigation at 10× the speed.
  • Brief drafting — first-draft generation with jurisdictional awareness.
  • Client intake — triaging matters and estimating complexity.

Tools shipping now: Harvey · CoCounsel (Thomson Reuters) · Lexis+ AI · Paxton AI · Hebbia · Spellbook · Ironclad AI

Lawyer hallucinations of non-existent case citations are a published professional-conduct issue. Citation checkers and retrieval-grounded answers are now mandatory; confidence is on verified-retrieval systems, not pure LLMs.

💬 Customer Support shipped

Where "agentic AI" first earned its keep in the enterprise. Ticket volume handled by AI went from ~10% (2023) to 50%+ (2026) at leading companies.

What AI is doing

  • Tier-1 resolution — returns, status checks, FAQs answered end-to-end by agents.
  • Agent-assist copilot — summarising conversations, drafting replies, pulling account context.
  • Voice / IVR replacement — realtime speech agents replacing phone trees.
  • Escalation classification — routing complex cases to the right human specialist.

Tools shipping now: Sierra · Intercom Fin · Decagon · Ada · Zendesk AI · Salesforce Agentforce · Parloa

Benchmarks like Sierra's own τ-bench and τ2-bench track this domain specifically. Resolution rates are measurable; regressions can be caught in CI.

🎨 Creative & Media shipped

Image and voice are mainstream consumer tools. Video and music crossed into production-usable in 2025. Design tools have AI baked in.

What AI is doing

  • Image generation — Midjourney, DALL-E, Flux, Ideogram. Now integrated into Photoshop, Figma, Canva.
  • Video generation — Sora, Veo, Runway Gen, Kling. Sub-minute clips are production-quality; long-form still hard.
  • Voice / audio — ElevenLabs TTS and voice cloning; realtime speech-to-speech (GPT-4o realtime, Sesame).
  • Music — Suno, Udio generate full tracks from text; licensing debates ongoing.
  • Design copilots — Figma Make, Adobe Firefly, Canva Magic Studio inside existing tools.

Tools shipping now: Midjourney · DALL-E 3 · Flux (Black Forest Labs) · Ideogram · Sora · Runway Gen-3 / Gen-4 · Kling · ElevenLabs · Suno · Udio · Adobe Firefly · Figma AI

Open litigation (NYT v. OpenAI, RIAA v. Suno/Udio) on training data is unresolved. Commercial users operate under provider indemnities. Watermarking / provenance (C2PA) is improving but not ubiquitous.

🏦 Finance & Banking emerging

Internal deployment is heavy; customer-facing is conservative. Research and ops are transforming faster than customer interaction.

What AI is doing

  • Document / KYC processing — extracting structured data from contracts, financials, ID documents.
  • Equity research synthesis — summarising earnings calls, 10-Ks, news flow.
  • Fraud detection — ML (not always generative) on transactions; LLMs layer on top for case narratives.
  • Trading ops & quant research — code generation, data pipeline scaffolding.
  • Wealth-mgmt copilots — advisor assistants drafting client communications.

Tools shipping now: JPMorgan LLM Suite · Bloomberg GPT · Morgan Stanley AI Assistant · FactSet AI · Hebbia · AlphaSense · Ramp Intelligence

Direct-to-retail AI financial advice is constrained by FINRA / MiFID / fiduciary duty. Most customer-visible AI is a chatbot layered on top of existing products; the real value is internal productivity.

📚 Education emerging

Students are ahead of institutions. Platform-scale tutoring exists and works; the harder question is what to do with it in classrooms.

What AI is doing

  • Tutoring — conversation-based help, socratic prompts, worked-example generation.
  • Language learning — Duolingo Max, Babbel AI — conversation practice with an LLM.
  • Grading assistance — essay and code grading with rubric alignment (teacher verifies).
  • Curriculum & lesson planning — teachers generate tailored materials in minutes.
  • Research writing assistance — the most common (and most contested) student use.

Tools shipping now: Khanmigo (Khan Academy) · Duolingo Max · Synthesis Tutor · MagicSchool · SchoolAI · Perplexity Edu

Detection of AI-written essays is unreliable. Schools are mostly pivoting to in-class assessment, oral defence, and process-based grading rather than relying on detection tools.

📣 Marketing & Sales shipped

From content generation (saturated) to outbound SDR agents (contested) to personalization engines (mature).

What AI is doing

  • Content at scale — blog posts, ad copy, email sequences.
  • Outbound SDR agents — autonomous prospecting, research, and first-touch outreach.
  • Personalized campaigns — dynamic copy and imagery per-segment or per-user.
  • CRM copilots — meeting notes, next-step suggestions, pipeline hygiene.
  • Attribution & analytics — natural-language querying of BI data.

Tools shipping now: Jasper · Copy.ai · Artisan · 11x · Salesforce Einstein / Agentforce · HubSpot Breeze · Gong · Regie.ai

AI-written cold outreach has badly eroded reply rates; most B2B inboxes now filter aggressive automation. The current arms race is deliverability and personalisation depth, not volume.

🔬 Science & Research emerging

The under-hyped superpower — see Vol III Ch 19 for the deep dive. Structural biology, weather, materials, and mathematics have all been visibly rewritten.

What AI is doing

  • Structural biology — AlphaFold 3 is standard infrastructure in drug discovery.
  • Weather & climate — GraphCast / GenCast match operational forecasters at <1% the compute.
  • Materials discovery — GNoME proposed ~380K stable crystals; A-Lab autonomously synthesises.
  • AI-for-math — AlphaProof reached IMO silver. Lean + LLM workflows for working mathematicians.
  • Literature synthesis — researcher copilots for paper discovery, summary, and critique.

Tools shipping now: AlphaFold 3 · Boltz-1 (open) · RFdiffusion · GraphCast · Isomorphic Labs · Recursion · Elicit · Undermind · Scite.ai · Consensus

AI-generated papers have flooded predatory journals and raised retraction rates. Scientific community now treats AI-assisted writing as acceptable; AI-generated results require explicit disclosure. Reproducibility still load-bearing.

📰 Journalism & Writing shipped

Reporters use AI daily. Newsrooms vary from "banned for final copy" to "integrated across workflow." The job changed faster than the job description.

What AI is doing

  • Interview transcription — the universal first use: Otter, Granola, Zoom AI.
  • Research synthesis — reading a thousand documents for a single story.
  • Drafting & editing — first drafts, headline variants, SEO optimisation.
  • Translation — wire stories in dozens of languages with minimal editorial touch.
  • Investigative data-mining — OSINT workflows for international reporting.

Tools shipping now: Otter.ai · Granola · Rev · ChatGPT / Claude (general) · Perplexity · NYT Semafor's AI tooling

Most major publishers have licensing deals (NYT ex.) or active litigation with AI labs. Editorial policy: AI can assist drafting but not source verification; all facts must be human-confirmed.

🤖 Robotics & Manufacturing emerging

Foundation policies reached physical robots in 2024-2025. Humanoids went from demo to limited deployment. See Vol III Ch 07 for the architecture.

What AI is doing

  • Foundation robot policies — one brain, many robot bodies (RT-2, π0, GR00T).
  • Warehouse pick & pack — dense bin-picking at scale.
  • Quality inspection — vision models on production lines.
  • Humanoid factory pilots — Figure, Agility (Digit), 1X, Apptronik.
  • Autonomous delivery / forklift — warehouse logistics, last-mile in controlled environments.

Companies shipping now: Physical Intelligence (π) · NVIDIA GR00T · Figure · Agility Robotics · 1X · Apptronik · Covariant (Amazon-acquired) · Symbotic · Dexterity

"Humanoid in the home" is still primarily demo reel. Factory pilots at BMW, Mercedes, and Figure's customers are real but narrow. Dexterity (shoe-tying, USB-insert) and long-horizon tasks remain unsolved.

🛒 Retail & E-commerce shipped

Personalization always was ML; LLMs added conversation. Shopping assistants and product Q&A now ship in every major storefront.

What AI is doing

  • Product discovery assistants — "find me jeans that fit a 32-30 and aren't skinny."
  • Review summarisation — aggregating and synthesising long review threads.
  • Visual search — "show me more like this image."
  • Personalization engines — long-standing ML, now LLM-augmented for content generation.
  • Returns-reason classification — auto-tagging returns for downstream supply-chain ops.

Tools shipping now: Amazon Rufus · Shopify Sidekick · eBay's AI listing tool · Walmart Sparky · Mercari AI Assist

Consumer trust in AI-written product descriptions and synthesised reviews is low; authenticity signals (human reviewer verification, seller video) have become the marketing counter-move.

🛡️ Defense & Intelligence emerging

Dual-use adoption is heavy. The frontier here is classified; what's public is the tip of a very large iceberg.

What AI is doing

  • OSINT synthesis — summarising open-source intelligence feeds at scale.
  • Imagery & signals analysis — satellite / drone feed ML has been standard for a decade; LLMs now write the analyst reports.
  • Wargaming & simulation — scenario generation, red-team simulation.
  • Autonomous drone systems — computer-vision-guided, increasingly agentic.
  • Logistics & maintenance — predictive maintenance, supply-chain optimisation for defence assets.

Companies visible now: Palantir AIP · Anduril · Helsing · Scale AI Defense · Shield AI · Rebellion Defense

The lethal-autonomy debate is live. Most frontier labs (Anthropic, OpenAI, Google DeepMind) have usage policies that permit defence-industry customers but restrict autonomous targeting; specifics vary and shift.

🌾 Agriculture emerging

Computer vision and robotics matter more here than LLMs. Still, the field is adopting fast — farmers get paid based on output, and AI is measurable.

What AI is doing

  • Satellite / drone crop monitoring — early detection of disease, water stress, nutrient gaps.
  • Autonomous weeders & sprayers — per-plant chemical application ("see & spray") reducing herbicide use ~90%.
  • Yield prediction — integrating weather, soil, and historical data for planting & harvest timing.
  • Autonomous tractors — level-4 autonomy on farm roads and fields.
  • Livestock health — CV on barn cameras for early illness detection.

Tools shipping now: John Deere See & Spray · Climate FieldView · Carbon Robotics · Monarch Tractor · Taranis

Commodity-crop farms (US, Brazil, Canada) adopt fastest due to scale economics. Smallholder adoption in the Global South is gated by cost and connectivity.

🏛️ Government & Public Sector experimental

Procurement cycles are long, failure is costly, and citizen-facing AI is politically fraught. Internal productivity deployments are growing; direct-to-citizen is cautious.

What AI is doing

  • Public-comment summarisation — agencies synthesise thousands of citizen comments on proposed rules.
  • Benefits processing — unemployment, disability, tax-return triage.
  • Translation services — municipal documents in dozens of languages.
  • Open-records Q&A — search/Q&A over public records, regulations, legislation.
  • Evaluator-of-record roles — UK AISI, US AISI do pre-deployment safety testing of frontier models.

Deployments visible now: UK AISI / US AISI (regulator-side) · GSA + 18F (US digital services) · Singapore Open Gov · various city pilots (Boston, Amsterdam, Helsinki)

High-profile failures have made governments risk-averse for public-facing AI. Most deployments are internal staff productivity; consumer-facing systems are narrowly scoped with explicit human override.

⚡ Energy & Utilities emerging

AI runs the electric grid that powers AI. Operational ML is decades old here; generative AI is newer and narrower.

What AI is doing

  • Grid optimization — demand forecasting, unit commitment, renewable integration.
  • Predictive maintenance — turbines, transformers, pipelines.
  • Exploration data analysis — seismic interpretation (oil & gas), mineral exploration.
  • Weather-driven forecasting — consumption prediction tied to GraphCast-class models.
  • Nuclear permit / plant ops support — document Q&A, compliance.

Deployments visible now: NVIDIA Earth-2 · Google DeepMind + BSA wind-farm forecasting · Utilidata · GridX · Enel / Iberdrola internal tooling

AI's own power demand is now a direct input to the industry. Hyperscaler nuclear PPAs (Microsoft/Three Mile Island, Meta/Amazon SMR deals) signalled the scale of AI's electricity bill.

Cross-cutting takeaways

  1. Internal > external. The biggest productivity wins in 2026 are employees using AI day-to-day. Customer-facing AI is harder and riskier.
  2. Copilot > autopilot. Every domain with liability (medical, legal, financial, defense) keeps a licensed human in the loop.
  3. Domain specificity matters. General LLMs are the substrate; real deployments layer retrieval, verification, guardrails, and domain data.
  4. Verification is the bottleneck. Capability is abundant; reliable capability is not. Evals, traces, and human-in-the-loop are load-bearing (Ch 11).
  5. Adoption is faster in industries with clear metrics. Customer support has ticket-close-rates; coding has test pass-rates; medicine has outcomes. These are the fastest-adopting.
