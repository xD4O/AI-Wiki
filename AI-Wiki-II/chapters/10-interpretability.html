CHAPTER 10 Part IV · Production

Interpretability & Explainability

What's actually going on inside a transformer? Features, circuits, sparse autoencoders, steering. A practitioner-level tour of mechanistic interpretability.

10.1 Why Interpretability Matters

If a model confidently claims a drug is safe, or a contract clause is standard, or a system log shows no attack — we would like to know why. Interpretability aims at three audiences:

  • Safety. Detect deception, hidden goals, jailbreak activation before deployment.
  • Debugging. When the model is wrong, what's firing? Can we turn the bug off?
  • Trust. Regulators, doctors, auditors want mechanistic explanations, not vibes.

10.2 Attention Visualizations

Attention heatmaps — "this word attends to that word" — were the first interpretability tool and are still the most common. They are useful but controversial: high attention weight does not cleanly mean "this token caused the output." Treat attention as a hint, not a proof.

10.3 Probing & the Linear Representation Hypothesis

Train a small linear classifier to read a concept off intermediate activations. If it works — "this layer encodes sentiment," "this layer knows factuality" — you have evidence the concept is represented linearly. This is the linear representation hypothesis: the model packs human-meaningful concepts as directions in activation space.

10.4 Sparse Autoencoders & Features

Internal activations are polysemantic — a single neuron often participates in many concepts. The 2023–2024 breakthrough was using sparse autoencoders (SAEs) to decompose activations into a larger dictionary of features that each fire rarely but monosemantically.

An SAE is trained to reconstruct activations \(a \in \mathbb{R}^d\) as a sparse combination of \(n \gg d\) features:

$$a \approx \sum_i z_i(a) \, v_i \qquad \text{with } z_i(a) \geq 0, \text{ sparse}$$

Anthropic's Scaling Monosemanticity work showed millions of interpretable features in Claude 3 Sonnet.

10.5 Circuits

A circuit is a minimal subgraph of components (attention heads, MLP neurons, SAE features) that together implement a behavior. The classic example is the "IOI" (Indirect Object Identification) circuit in GPT-2, which uses a small set of heads to track subject and object across a sentence.

Tools: TransformerLens for the model, nnsight for activation patching at arbitrary depths, SAELens for working with pretrained SAEs. The workflow: ablate a component, measure the performance drop, find the minimal set.

10.6 Activation Steering & Model Surgery

Once you have a feature vector \(v\), you can add it to the residual stream at inference time to bias the model toward that feature. No retraining — just an intervention.

import nnsight

model = nnsight.LanguageModel("meta-llama/Llama-3.1-8B")

with model.trace("The weather today is") as tracer:
    # at layer 15, add a "happy" steering vector scaled by 3.0
    model.model.layers[15].output[0][:, -1, :] += 3.0 * happy_vector
    out = model.lm_head.output.save()
print(model.tokenizer.decode(out.argmax(-1)[0]))

10.7 Chain-of-Thought Faithfulness

Models don't always say what they're thinking

A growing body of work (Turpin 2023, Anthropic 2024, 2025) shows that a model's chain-of-thought is often not the real cause of its answer. The model may be post-hoc rationalizing a decision already determined by earlier layers. Don't equate CoT with interpretability — it's a hint at best.
